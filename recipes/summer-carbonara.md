# Summer Carbonara with Bacon, Beans & Courgette

A carbonara that doesn't pretend summer doesn't exist. The classic egg-and-cheese sauce is all here — rich, glossy, clinging to every strand — but the addition of charred courgette ribbons and barely-blanched green beans lifts the whole thing into something lighter and brighter than the Roman original. The vegetables aren't an afterthought. They're seared hard so they bring their own flavour to the plate rather than just sitting there being virtuous.

## Ingredients

### Pasta

- 400g dried spaghetti (or rigatoni if you prefer something the sauce can grip)
- fine sea salt for the water (be generous — it should taste like the sea)

### Carbonara Sauce

- 4 large egg yolks
- 2 whole eggs
- 80g Pecorino Romano, finely grated (use a microplane)
- 30g Parmesan, finely grated
- generous crack of black pepper — at least 1/2 tsp, coarsely ground

### Bacon & Vegetables

- 200g smoked streaky bacon (or pancetta), cut into 1cm lardons
- 2 medium courgettes
- 150g fine green beans, trimmed and cut into 4cm lengths
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 clove garlic, lightly crushed with the flat of a knife (removed later)

### Finishing

- extra Pecorino for the table
- black pepper
- good olive oil
- small handful of basil leaves (optional, but this is a summer dish)

## Instructions

1. **Make the sauce first.** In a bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, whole eggs, grated Pecorino, Parmesan, and black pepper until smooth and creamy. The mixture should be thick — almost like a loose paste. Set aside. This is the only chance you have to get this right before the chaos of tossing it all together, so do it now while things are calm.

2. **Prep the courgettes.** Cut the courgettes lengthways into thin ribbons using a Y-peeler or mandoline — about 2-3mm thick. You want strips, not coins. They should be thin enough to char quickly but sturdy enough to hold their shape when tossed with the pasta.

3. **Blanch the beans.** Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to the boil (this is also your pasta water). Drop in the green beans and cook for 2 minutes — they should be bright green and still have bite. Scoop them out with a slotted spoon into a bowl. Keep the water boiling for the pasta.

4. **Cook the pasta.** Add the spaghetti to the same boiling water. Cook for 1 minute less than the packet says. You want it properly al dente because it will finish cooking in the pan. Before draining, ladle out a mugful of starchy pasta water — you will need it.

5. **Render the bacon.** While the pasta cooks, place the lardons in a large, cold frying pan. Set over medium heat. Cook slowly for 6-8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the fat renders out and the edges turn deep gold and crisp. Starting in a cold pan means the fat renders gradually rather than the outside burning while the centre stays soft. Remove the bacon to a plate, leaving the fat in the pan.

6. **Char the courgettes.** Turn the heat to high. Add 1 tbsp olive oil to the bacon fat. Lay the courgette ribbons in the pan in a single layer (you may need to do this in batches). Cook undisturbed for 60-90 seconds until the undersides have dark char marks. Flip briefly, then transfer to the plate with the bacon. Drop the crushed garlic clove into the pan for 30 seconds, then discard it — you just want a whisper of garlic in the fat.

7. **Bring it all together.** This is the critical 60 seconds. Turn the heat to low. Add the drained pasta to the pan with the garlicky fat. Toss for 30 seconds. Remove the pan from the heat entirely. Pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing constantly and vigorously with tongs. Add pasta water a splash at a time — about 3-4 tablespoons — until the sauce becomes glossy, creamy, and coats every strand. The residual heat of the pasta cooks the eggs into a sauce. If the pan is too hot, you get scrambled eggs. If it's too cool, the sauce won't emulsify. Off the heat with vigorous tossing is the sweet spot.

8. **Add the vegetables.** Fold in the crispy bacon, charred courgette ribbons, and blanched green beans. Toss gently — just enough to distribute everything without breaking the courgettes.

9. **Plate.** Twirl into warmed bowls using tongs. Finish with more grated Pecorino, a crack of black pepper, a thread of olive oil, and basil leaves if using. Serve immediately — carbonara waits for no one.

## Notes

- **The heat is everything.** Carbonara sauce is an emulsion, not a cooked egg dish. The pan must be off the heat when the egg mixture goes in. Toss aggressively — the friction and residual heat are all you need. If it looks too thick, add more pasta water. If it looks too thin, keep tossing — it will come together.
- **Pasta water is the secret ingredient.** The starch in the water is what binds the sauce. Without it, you get clumpy cheese and loose eggs. With it, you get silk. Save more than you think you need.
- **Pecorino over Parmesan.** Traditional carbonara uses Pecorino Romano for its salty, sharp punch. The small amount of Parmesan added here rounds out the flavour without losing that edge. Do not substitute with pre-grated cheese from a tub — it contains anti-caking agents that prevent it from melting smoothly.
- **Charring the courgettes.** This is the step that makes the vegetables taste like they belong in the dish rather than being health-food penance. High heat, single layer, don't touch them. The char adds a smokiness that pairs with the bacon fat.
- **Timing.** Get everything prepped before you cook the pasta. Once the spaghetti is in the water, you have 10 minutes to render bacon, char courgettes, and drain pasta. Read the recipe through once before you start so the final assembly feels fluid rather than frantic.
