# Salted Chocolate Chunk Shortbread Cookies

Alison Roman's wildly popular shortbread — a rethink of the chocolate chip cookie that fixes everything she finds wrong with the original. "Too sweet, too soft, or with too much chocolate, there's a lot of room for improvement," she writes. These use salted butter (it has a slightly different flavour and a deeper saltiness than using just salt), just enough flour to hold together, and light brown sugar to suggest a chocolate chip cookie without actually being one. The slice-and-bake format means the dough lives in your freezer, ready to go.

## Ingredients

### Dough

- 1 cup plus 2 tbsp (255g) salted butter, cold (room temperature if using a hand mixer), cut into 1cm pieces
- 1/2 cup (100g) granulated sugar
- 1/4 cup (55g) light brown sugar
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 2 1/2 cups (325g) all-purpose flour
- 170g semi-sweet or bittersweet dark chocolate, chopped into chunks (not too fine — you want chunks, not little shards)

### Finishing

- 1 large egg, beaten
- demerara sugar, for rolling
- flaky sea salt, for sprinkling

## Instructions

1. **Cream the butter.** Line two rimmed baking sheets with parchment paper. Using a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment, beat the butter, both sugars, and vanilla on medium-high until super light and fluffy — 3 to 5 minutes for a stand mixer, 6 to 8 for a hand mixer. Scrape down the sides of the bowl with a spatula.

2. **Add the flour and chocolate.** With the mixer on low, slowly add the flour, followed by the chocolate chunks, and mix just to blend. If necessary, knead the dough with your hands to make sure the flour is totally incorporated. The dough should be smooth and feel like Play-Doh with no pockets of flour.

3. **Shape the logs.** Divide the dough in half, placing each half on a large piece of cling film. Fold the plastic over to cover the dough, then form each half into a 15cm (6-inch) log, about 5-6cm (2 to 2 1/4 inches) in diameter. Rolling it on the counter helps smooth it out, but don't worry about getting it perfect. Don't be afraid to make them compact — shortbread is supposed to be dense. Chill until totally firm, about 2 hours.

4. **Prep for baking.** Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Brush the outside of the logs with the beaten egg and roll them in demerara sugar — this is what gives you those crisp, caramelised edges.

5. **Slice.** Using a serrated knife, carefully cut each log into 1cm (1/2-inch) thick rounds. If you hit a chocolate chunk, slowly saw back and forth through it. If cookies break or fall apart, just press them back together — the dough is very forgiving. Place on the prepared baking sheets about 2-3cm apart (they won't spread much). Sprinkle each round with flaky sea salt.

6. **Bake.** 12 to 15 minutes, until the edges are just beginning to brown. Let cool slightly before eating.

## Notes

- **Salted butter is intentional.** Alison specifically calls for salted butter here — it gives a deeper, more rounded saltiness than adding salt separately. This is the one recipe where salted butter is the right choice.
- **Chocolate chunks, not chips.** Chop a bar yourself. You want irregular, jagged pieces that create pockets of melted chocolate and shards of solid chocolate throughout. Chocolate chips are designed not to melt and will give you a completely different cookie.
- **Make ahead.** The dough keeps tightly wrapped for up to 1 week in the fridge or 1 month in the freezer. Slice and bake from frozen — just add a minute or two to the bake time. Baked cookies keep in an airtight container for 5 days.
- **Don't overbake.** Pull them when the edges are barely golden. They firm up as they cool. Overbaked shortbread is dry and crumbly rather than sandy and rich.
