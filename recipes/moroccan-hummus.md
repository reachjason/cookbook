# Moroccan Hummus

A silky, deeply flavored hummus inspired by the spice markets of Marrakech. The key to restaurant-quality texture is properly cooking dried chickpeas until they practically dissolve, removing the skins, and using ice-cold water to emulsify the tahini. Blooming the spices in warm olive oil unlocks aromatics that ground cumin from a jar can never match.

## Ingredients

### Chickpeas
- 1 cup dried chickpeas
- 1 tsp baking soda (for soaking)
- 1/2 tsp baking soda (for cooking)
- 4 cups water (for Instant Pot)

### Hummus Base
- 1/2 cup high-quality tahini (well-stirred)
- 3 tbsp fresh lemon juice (about 1 large lemon)
- 1 small clove garlic, finely grated on a microplane
- 3-4 tbsp ice-cold water
- 3/4 tsp fine sea salt, to taste

### Moroccan Spice Bloom
- 3 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1/2 tsp coriander seeds
- 1/2 tsp sweet paprika
- 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp ground ginger
- pinch of cayenne
- 1/2 preserved lemon, pulp discarded, rind finely minced (or zest of 1 lemon + pinch of salt)

### Garnish
- extra virgin olive oil
- pinch of smoked paprika
- toasted pine nuts
- fresh cilantro leaves
- dusting of ground cumin

## Instructions

1. **Soak the chickpeas.** Place dried chickpeas in a large bowl with 1 tsp baking soda. Cover with several inches of water. Soak overnight or at least 12 hours. Drain and rinse thoroughly.

2. **Pressure cook.** Add soaked chickpeas, 1/2 tsp baking soda, and 4 cups water to the Instant Pot. Cook on high pressure for 25 minutes. Allow natural release for 15 minutes, then quick release any remaining pressure.

3. **Test and skin.** The chickpeas should be very soft — they should smash easily between two fingers with zero resistance. If not, cook 5 more minutes on high pressure. Drain, reserving 1/4 cup cooking liquid. While still warm, rub chickpeas gently between your hands or in a clean towel to loosen the skins. Remove and discard as many skins as you can. This is the step that separates good hummus from extraordinary hummus.

4. **Build the tahini base.** In a food processor, combine tahini, lemon juice, grated garlic, and salt. Process for 90 seconds until the mixture looks pale, thick, and whipped. With the processor running, slowly drizzle in the ice-cold water, one tablespoon at a time, until the mixture is very smooth and lighter in color.

5. **Add chickpeas.** Add the warm, skinned chickpeas to the food processor. Process for 3-4 minutes straight, scraping down the sides once. You are looking for a texture so smooth it looks like thick cream. If needed, add reserved cooking liquid one tablespoon at a time to reach the desired consistency. Season with additional salt and lemon juice to taste.

6. **Bloom the spices.** In a small pan over medium-low heat, warm the olive oil. Add cumin seeds and coriander seeds. Toast for 60-90 seconds until fragrant and the cumin seeds darken slightly. Remove from heat. Stir in paprika, cinnamon, ginger, cayenne, and preserved lemon rind. The residual heat will bloom the ground spices. Let cool slightly.

7. **Plate.** Transfer hummus to a shallow bowl. Use the back of a spoon to create a swooping well in the center. Spoon the bloomed spice oil into the well. Scatter with toasted pine nuts, cilantro leaves, a dusting of cumin, and a pinch of smoked paprika. Finish with a final drizzle of olive oil.

## Notes

- **The skins matter.** Removing chickpea skins is tedious but non-negotiable for michelin-level texture. Budget 10 minutes for this step.
- **Tahini quality.** Use a runny, well-emulsified tahini (Soom or Al Arz are excellent). Thick, separated tahini will yield a gritty hummus.
- **Ice water trick.** Cold water emulsifies with tahini to create a fluffy, light base before the chickpeas even go in. Process the tahini base first — always.
- **Make ahead.** Hummus keeps 4-5 days refrigerated. Bring to room temperature and refresh with a drizzle of olive oil before serving. Bloom the spice oil fresh.
- **Preserved lemons.** If you can find them, they add an irreplaceable fermented depth. Beldi-style Moroccan preserved lemons are ideal.
