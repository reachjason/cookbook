# Baked Honey Butter Salmon with Lemon & Garlic

This is the recipe you reach for on a Wednesday night when you want something that looks and tastes like you tried, but didn't actually suffer. The technique is simple: a hot oven, a generous honey-butter glaze brushed on twice, and the discipline to pull the fish the moment it turns opaque at the edges while the centre still has the faintest blush of translucence. That carryover heat finishes the job. The garlic is cooked just enough to lose its bite but not so much that it disappears. The lemon does all the work of cutting through the richness so nothing else needs to.

## Ingredients

### Salmon

- 4 skin-on salmon fillets (about 180g each, 2-3cm thick)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- fine sea salt
- freshly ground black pepper

### Honey Butter Glaze

- 60g unsalted butter, melted
- 2 tbsp honey (runny, not set)
- 4 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 1 tsp lemon zest (zest first, then juice the lemon)
- 1/2 tsp flaky sea salt
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- pinch of cayenne (optional — just enough warmth to keep things interesting)

### Finishing

- 1 tbsp cold butter, cut into 4 small pieces
- fresh lemon wedges
- flaky sea salt
- chopped fresh parsley or dill

## Instructions

1. **Prep the salmon.** Remove the fillets from the fridge 15 minutes before cooking — cold fish in a hot oven cooks unevenly, leaving you with a dry exterior and raw centre. Pat the fillets thoroughly dry with kitchen paper. This matters more than any seasoning you add. Moisture on the surface steams instead of caramelising. Season both sides with fine sea salt and black pepper.

2. **Make the glaze.** In a small bowl, stir together the melted butter, honey, minced garlic, lemon juice, lemon zest, flaky salt, black pepper, and cayenne. The mixture should taste punchy and slightly too intense on its own — it will mellow against the richness of the salmon. Set aside.

3. **Heat the oven.** Set to 220°C (425°F) fan. Place a rimmed baking sheet or cast-iron skillet in the oven while it preheats — starting with a hot surface gives the skin-side a head start and prevents the fish from sticking.

4. **Sear the base.** Carefully remove the hot tray from the oven. Drizzle with 1 tbsp olive oil. Lay the salmon fillets skin-side down on the hot surface. You should hear an immediate sizzle — if you don't, the tray wasn't hot enough. Brush the tops generously with half the honey butter glaze, making sure the garlic is distributed evenly across all four fillets.

5. **Bake.** Return to the oven. Bake for 8 minutes. Remove the tray and brush with the remaining glaze — this second coat builds a lacquered, sticky finish that a single application can never achieve. Return to the oven for 4-6 minutes more. The total time depends on thickness: 12-14 minutes for 2cm fillets, up to 16 for very thick cuts. The salmon is done when the top is caramelised and golden, the flesh flakes easily at the edges when pressed with a fork, and the very centre still looks slightly translucent. It will continue cooking for 2-3 minutes after leaving the oven.

6. **Rest and finish.** Transfer the fillets to warmed plates. While the fish rests, scrape any caramelised glaze and garlic bits from the tray into a small bowl — this is liquid gold, do not leave it behind. Stir in 1 tbsp cold butter to emulsify into a glossy pan sauce. Spoon this over each fillet. Finish with a squeeze of fresh lemon, a scatter of herbs, and a pinch of flaky sea salt.

## Notes

- **Don't overcook it.** This is the only rule that matters. Salmon continues cooking after it leaves the oven. Pull it when the centre is still slightly translucent — it will carry over to a perfect medium by the time it reaches the table. Overcooked salmon is dry, chalky, and no amount of butter sauce will rescue it. Use a thermometer if you're unsure: 50°C (122°F) internal for medium, which will rise to 55°C (131°F) during rest.
- **Skin-side down, always.** The skin acts as a barrier between the flesh and the heat source, insulating the bottom of the fillet so it cooks gently while the top caramelises under the glaze. It also means the skin crisps against the hot tray. If your guests don't eat the skin, that's their loss.
- **Honey burns fast.** The high oven temperature means the glaze can go from golden to bitter quickly in the last few minutes. Check at the 12-minute mark. If the tops are deeply caramelised but the fish needs more time, tent loosely with foil.
- **The double-glaze technique.** Brushing once gives you flavour. Brushing twice — once before the oven and once partway through — gives you a lacquered, sticky coating with real depth. The first layer caramelises into the fish. The second stays glossy and saucy.
- **Garlic.** Minced, not sliced, not crushed. Minced garlic melts into the butter and distributes evenly. Sliced garlic would burn at this oven temperature. If you find raw garlic too sharp, cook it in the melted butter for 30 seconds before adding the honey and lemon.
- **Sides.** This pairs well with something that can absorb the sauce — steamed rice, crushed new potatoes, or a simple green salad dressed with lemon and olive oil. Anything too complex will compete with the glaze.
