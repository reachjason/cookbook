# Raan-e-Dumpukht (Instant Pot)

A slow-braised whole lamb shoulder in the Mughlai *dum* style — fall-off-the-bone tender, deeply spiced, finished with saffron and cream. *Dumpukht* translates roughly to "breath-cooked": the pot is sealed with a rope of dough so nothing escapes, and the lamb cooks in its own perfumed steam for hours. The Instant Pot is an unlikely but excellent stand-in — its pressure seal does exactly what the dough once did, and a natural release lets the meat relax into the braise instead of tightening against it. You still roll a dough rope around the rim; partly for insurance, partly because it feels right.

## Ingredients

### Lamb and Marinade

- 1 kg bone-in lamb shoulder
- 200g full-fat Greek yoghurt
- 12 garlic cloves, made into paste
- 50g fresh ginger, made into paste
- 2 tbsp raw papaya paste (meat tenderiser)
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp Kashmiri red chilli powder
- 1 1/2 tbsp garam masala
- 1 tbsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground fennel seeds
- 1 tsp ground black pepper
- 1/2 tsp ground mace (javitri)
- 1/3 tsp ground nutmeg
- 2 tsp salt

### Tempering and Braise

- 4 tbsp ghee
- 6 green cardamom pods, crushed
- 2 black cardamom pods
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 cloves
- 100g crispy fried onions (birista)
- 4 green chillies, slit
- 100ml water or lamb stock

### Finishing

- 1 pinch saffron strands
- 3 tbsp warm whole milk (for the saffron)
- 60ml heavy cream
- 1 tsp rose water
- 1 tsp kewra water (screwpine)
- 15g fresh coriander, chopped
- 10g fresh mint leaves, chopped
- 100g whole-wheat dough (for sealing the lid)

## Instructions

1. **Bloom the saffron.** Soak the saffron strands in 3 tbsp warm whole milk and set aside for at least 15 minutes. This draws out the colour and aroma — dropping dry strands into the braise wastes both.

2. **Make the marinade.** In a large bowl combine the yoghurt, garlic paste, ginger paste, papaya paste, lemon juice, Kashmiri chilli, garam masala, coriander, cumin, fennel, black pepper, mace, nutmeg, and salt. Mix to a smooth paste. It should smell fierce and look almost too red — it will mellow enormously over the cook.

3. **Score and marinate the lamb.** Make deep slashes all over the lamb shoulder — right down to the bone. These are not decorative. They let the marinade penetrate past the surface and give the papaya enzymes access to the connective tissue. Rub the marinade thoroughly into every cut and all over the surface. Cover and refrigerate for a minimum of 6 hours, ideally overnight. This is the difference between good raan and transcendent raan.

4. **Sear the lamb.** Set the Instant Pot to SAUTÉ — HIGH. Add the ghee and let it shimmer. Sear the marinated lamb on all sides until deeply browned, about 8 minutes per side. Don't rush this — the fond you're building is the backbone of the sauce. Remove and set aside.

5. **Bloom the whole spices.** In the same pot, still on SAUTÉ, add the green cardamom, black cardamom, cinnamon, bay leaves, and cloves. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant. Whole spices need hot fat to release their oils — skipping this leaves them tasting like twigs in the finished dish.

6. **Build the braising liquid.** Add the crispy fried onions and stir briefly. Pour in the water or lamb stock, scraping up all the fond from the bottom. Stir in the slit green chillies. Return the seared lamb to the pot, spooning any remaining marinade over the top.

7. **Add the finishing aromatics.** Drizzle the bloomed saffron milk, cream, rose water, and kewra water over the lamb. Scatter the chopped coriander and mint on top. Resist stirring — you want these to perfume the top of the lamb as it cooks.

8. **Seal the lid (dum style).** Roll the whole-wheat dough into a long rope and press it around the rim of the Instant Pot inner pot. This mimics the traditional dough-sealed dum. Lock the Instant Pot lid on top and set the valve to SEALING.

9. **Pressure cook.** Set to MEAT/STEW or PRESSURE COOK — HIGH for 65 minutes. Allow a full NATURAL RELEASE, about 20 minutes. Do not quick-release. Dropping the pressure fast shocks the meat fibres and tightens them; natural release lets the lamb relax in the braise. The lamb should be completely tender and pulling away from the bone.

10. **Rest and reduce.** Carefully lift the lamb onto a platter and tent loosely with foil. Switch the pot back to SAUTÉ and reduce the braising liquid by about half until it coats the back of a spoon, roughly 8-10 minutes. Taste and adjust salt.

11. **Serve.** Spoon the reduced sauce over the lamb. Garnish with a final pinch of saffron, fresh mint, toasted almond slivers, and a drizzle of cream. Serve with roomali roti, naan, or saffron pulao.

## Notes

- **The overnight marinate is not negotiable.** The papaya enzymes and yoghurt need hours to break down the collagen in the shoulder. Six is the minimum; overnight is the target. A two-hour marinate will give you a serviceable curry, not raan.
- **Why the dough seal.** The Instant Pot lid already seals perfectly — the dough rope is partly tradition, partly insurance against aroma loss at the hinge, and partly because the smell of lightly baked wheat becomes part of the finished dish. Skip it if you must, but you lose a layer.
- **Bloom the saffron, always.** Dropping dry strands into any hot liquid wastes most of their flavour compounds, which are fat- and water-soluble and need time to diffuse. A warm milk bath for 15 minutes unlocks the full red-gold colour and honeyed aroma.
- **Kashmiri chilli is for colour, not heat.** If you substitute regular chilli powder, halve the quantity or the dish will be punishing. The green chillies bring the actual heat.
- **Natural release matters.** Quick-releasing pressure on braised meat seizes the fibres and leaves the lamb stringy even after a long cook. The 20 minutes of natural release are part of the cooking time, not an inconvenience.
- **Make ahead.** Raan is better the next day. Cool completely, refrigerate in its sauce, and reheat gently in a covered pan with a splash of water or stock. The spices integrate overnight and the meat reabsorbs the braising liquid.
- **Lamb shoulder, not leg.** Leg is leaner and dries out over a long braise. Shoulder has the marbling and connective tissue that turn silky under pressure. If your butcher offers bone-in shoulder, take it — the bone adds body to the sauce.
