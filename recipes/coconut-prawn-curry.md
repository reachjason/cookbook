# Coconut Milk Prawn Curry

A refined South Indian-inspired prawn curry built on three layers of coconut — toasted, creamed, and tempered — that produces a sauce so layered it barely resembles the tin-and-paste versions most of us grew up on. The prawns are seared hard and fast, then folded in at the very end so they stay plump and sweet against the rich, fragrant gravy. Every element here is deliberate. This is a weeknight curry elevated to something worth remembering.

## Ingredients

### Prawns

- 500g large raw king prawns, shell-on (about 16-20)
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tbsp neutral oil (grapeseed or sunflower)

### Coconut Base

- 3 tbsp desiccated coconut
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1/2 tsp black peppercorns
- 400ml full-fat coconut milk (one tin, unshaken)
- 150ml water or prawn shell stock (see notes)

### Curry

- 3 tbsp coconut oil
- 1/2 tsp black mustard seeds
- 12-15 fresh curry leaves (not dried — they are not a substitute)
- 2 medium shallots, finely sliced into half-moons
- 4 cloves garlic, finely sliced
- 30g fresh ginger, peeled and cut into fine matchsticks
- 2 green chillies (bird's eye for heat, finger chillies for milder), slit lengthways
- 1/2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
- 1/4 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp fine sea salt, to taste
- 1 tbsp tamarind paste (or juice of 1 lime)

### Coconut Tempering (Tarka)

- 1 tbsp coconut oil
- 1/2 tsp black mustard seeds
- 8-10 fresh curry leaves
- 2 dried Kashmiri chillies, broken in half
- 1 tbsp fresh coconut shavings or desiccated coconut

### Finishing

- flaky sea salt
- 1 tbsp cold-pressed coconut oil (raw, unrefined)
- fresh coriander leaves
- 1 lime, cut into wedges

## Instructions

1. **Peel and prep the prawns.** Peel the prawns, keeping the tails intact. Devein by running a small knife along the back. Reserve the shells and heads. Toss the prawns with 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp turmeric. Refrigerate uncovered while you build the sauce — this seasons the prawns through and dries the surface for a better sear.

2. **Make a quick stock (optional but worth it).** Heat a dry pan over high heat. Add the reserved prawn shells and heads. Toast for 3-4 minutes, pressing them into the pan until they turn deep orange and smell intensely of the sea. Add 250ml water, simmer for 10 minutes, then strain. You'll use 150ml of this. This is the difference between a good curry and one that tastes like it came from somewhere with a pass.

3. **Toast the coconut spice paste.** In a dry frying pan over medium heat, add desiccated coconut, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, and black peppercorns. Toast for 2-3 minutes, shaking constantly, until the coconut is deep gold and the spices are fragrant. Transfer immediately to a spice grinder or mortar. Grind to a fine powder. This is the body of the sauce — it thickens the curry naturally without any flour or cream.

4. **Build the base.** Heat 3 tbsp coconut oil in a heavy-bottomed pan or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add mustard seeds. When they begin to pop and crackle (about 30 seconds), add curry leaves — stand back, they will spit. Stir for 10 seconds until the leaves are crisp and translucent. Add sliced shallots with a pinch of salt. Cook for 6-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply golden and sweet. Add garlic and ginger matchsticks. Cook for 2 minutes until softened but not browned. Add the slit green chillies, Kashmiri chilli powder, and turmeric. Stir for 30 seconds until the oil turns red-orange.

5. **Add the coconut.** Pour in the coconut milk — use the thick cream from the top of the tin first, stirring it into the aromatics until it splits slightly and the oil separates. This is what you want. Then add the thinner coconut water from the tin and the prawn shell stock (or plain water). Stir in the toasted coconut spice powder and tamarind paste. Bring to a gentle simmer. Cook uncovered for 12-15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce reduces by about a third and coats the back of a spoon. Taste and adjust salt and tamarind — the sauce should be rich, slightly tangy, and deeply aromatic.

6. **Sear the prawns.** While the sauce simmers, heat a separate pan to very high heat — almost smoking. Add 1 tbsp neutral oil. Lay the prawns flat in a single layer. Do not touch them for 60-90 seconds until the underside is deeply caramelised and pink-gold. Flip and cook for 30 seconds more. The prawns should be just barely cooked through — they'll finish in the sauce. Remove immediately.

7. **Bring it together.** Reduce the curry heat to low. Slide the seared prawns into the sauce. Fold gently — do not stir aggressively or the prawns will break. Let them sit in the sauce for 2 minutes, spooning curry over the top, until just heated through and glazed.

8. **Make the tempering.** In a small pan, heat 1 tbsp coconut oil over medium-high heat. Add mustard seeds and let them pop. Add curry leaves and broken dried chillies. Fry for 20 seconds until the curry leaves are crisp and the chillies darken. Add coconut shavings and toast for 15 seconds until golden. Remove from heat immediately.

9. **Plate.** Spoon the curry into warmed shallow bowls, arranging the prawns with the tails facing up. Pour the coconut tempering over the top — oil and all. Drizzle with a thread of raw coconut oil. Scatter with fresh coriander and a few flakes of sea salt. Serve with a lime wedge and steamed basmati or appam.

## Notes

- **Prawn quality matters more than anything else.** Use the largest, freshest raw prawns you can find. Frozen is fine — thaw slowly in the fridge overnight, never under warm water. Pre-cooked prawns will turn to rubber. This is non-negotiable.
- **The three coconuts.** The toasted coconut powder gives body. The coconut milk gives richness. The raw coconut oil drizzled at the end gives aroma. Each layer does a different job. Don't skip any of them.
- **Curry leaves.** Fresh curry leaves are essential — they provide a floral, almost citrus-like backbone that dried leaves cannot replicate. Find them at any Indian grocery. They freeze well in a zip-lock bag for months.
- **Searing the prawns separately.** This is the restaurant technique that most home recipes skip. Searing gives you Maillard flavour on the prawns while keeping them succulent. Adding raw prawns directly to simmering sauce steams them — edible, but not the same.
- **Tamarind balance.** The curry wants a gentle sourness to cut through the coconut fat. Add tamarind in small amounts and taste as you go. Too much will make the sauce sharp; too little and it will taste flat and one-dimensional.
- **Make ahead.** The sauce (without prawns) keeps for 2 days refrigerated. Sear the prawns fresh and fold them into the gently reheated sauce just before serving.
