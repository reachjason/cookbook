# Palak Paneer

The version most restaurants serve is a dull, army-green gravy with rubbery paneer cubes floating in it. This is not that. The secret is twofold: blanch the spinach for exactly 60 seconds to lock in a vivid emerald colour, and fry the paneer until it develops a golden crust that holds its shape in the sauce. A small amount of cream stirred in off the heat keeps the colour bright and adds a richness that makes the whole thing taste like it belongs on a tasting menu rather than a Tuesday night.

## Ingredients

### Spinach Base

- 500g fresh baby spinach (or mature spinach, tough stems removed)
- 4-5 cups water for blanching
- a large bowl of ice water

### Paneer

- 250g paneer, cut into 2cm cubes
- 1 tbsp ghee or neutral oil
- pinch of fine sea salt

### Curry Base

- 3 tbsp ghee (not oil — the flavour matters here)
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 medium onions, finely diced
- 1 tsp fine sea salt, to taste
- 6 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 20g fresh ginger, grated on a microplane
- 2 green chillies, finely chopped (seeds removed for milder heat)
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp garam masala
- 1/4 tsp ground turmeric
- 1/4 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
- 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped (or 200g tinned chopped tomatoes)

### Finishing

- 3 tbsp double cream (or cashew cream for dairy-alternative richness)
- 1 tsp dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi), crushed between your palms
- 1/2 tsp garam masala
- squeeze of lemon juice
- 1 tbsp cold butter (optional, but not really)

## Instructions

1. **Blanch the spinach.** Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Add all the spinach and push it under with a spoon. Blanch for exactly 60 seconds — no longer. Drain immediately and plunge into the bowl of ice water. This stops the cooking and locks in a colour so green it almost glows. Once cool, squeeze the spinach firmly in your fists to remove as much water as possible. You want it almost dry. Blend to a smooth puree with 2-3 tbsp water — just enough to get the blades moving. Set aside.

2. **Fry the paneer.** Heat 1 tbsp ghee in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat. Add the paneer cubes in a single layer with a pinch of salt. Fry undisturbed for 90 seconds until the bottom is deep golden. Turn and repeat on one or two more sides. You are not cooking the paneer through — you are building a crust that will hold up in the sauce. Transfer to a plate lined with kitchen paper.

3. **Build the base.** In a heavy-bottomed pan, heat 3 tbsp ghee over medium heat. Add cumin seeds and let them sizzle and darken for 30 seconds. Add diced onions and 1 tsp salt. Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onions are soft, golden, and starting to catch on the bottom of the pan. This patience is where all the sweetness in the sauce comes from. Add the garlic, ginger, and green chillies. Cook for 2 minutes until the raw smell disappears and the kitchen smells extraordinary.

4. **Add the spices and tomatoes.** Add ground coriander, cumin, garam masala, turmeric, and Kashmiri chilli powder. Stir constantly for 30 seconds — the spices should darken slightly and coat the onions in a rust-coloured paste. Add the chopped tomatoes. Cook for 5-6 minutes, mashing with the back of your spoon, until the tomatoes break down completely and the oil begins to separate from the masala. This is the signal that the base is ready.

5. **Add the spinach puree.** Pour the spinach puree into the masala base. Stir well to combine. Add 50-80ml water to reach your desired consistency — it should be the texture of a thick, pourable sauce, not a paste. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 4-5 minutes, stirring often, so the spinach absorbs the flavour of the masala.

6. **Finish the sauce.** Remove from heat. Stir in the cream — adding it off the heat prevents the sauce from turning a dull khaki. Crush the kasuri methi between your palms directly over the pan (this releases the oils) and stir through. Add the finishing garam masala and a squeeze of lemon juice. Taste and adjust salt. If you want a glossy, restaurant-quality finish, stir in 1 tbsp cold butter now — it emulsifies into the sauce and adds a sheen that cream alone cannot.

7. **Bring it together.** Gently fold the fried paneer cubes into the sauce. Let them sit for 2-3 minutes on very low heat so they warm through and absorb some of the gravy. Do not simmer aggressively or the spinach will lose its colour and the paneer will turn spongy.

8. **Plate.** Spoon into a warmed bowl. Drizzle with a thin line of cream. Serve immediately with hot naan, roti, or steamed basmati rice.

## Notes

- **The 60-second rule.** Overcooking spinach is the single most common mistake. Beyond 60 seconds in boiling water, the bright chlorophyll breaks down and you get the grey-green colour of every disappointing palak paneer you have ever eaten. Blanch fast, ice bath immediately.
- **Kasuri methi is non-negotiable.** Dried fenugreek leaves add a smoky, slightly bitter aroma that is the signature note of North Indian gravies. Crush them between your palms before adding — this releases the volatile oils. Available at any Indian grocery; keeps for months in an airtight jar.
- **Paneer texture.** Frying gives paneer a skin that resists becoming spongy in the sauce. If you skip this step, the cubes will absorb liquid and turn soft and characterless. Some people soak the fried paneer in warm salted water for 10 minutes before adding to the sauce — this keeps the inside creamy while the outside stays firm.
- **Cream off the heat.** Adding dairy to hot spinach turns it brown. Always take the pan off the heat before stirring in cream or butter. This is the restaurant secret for vivid green palak paneer.
- **Make ahead.** The spinach puree and masala base can be made a day ahead and refrigerated separately. Combine, add paneer, and finish just before serving — the colour will be even brighter than cooking it all in one go.
