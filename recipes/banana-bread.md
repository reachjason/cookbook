# Banana Bread

An easy way to use up the bananas on the countertop, or brown ones thrown in the back of the freezer. Don't overmix the ingredients, and make sure the bananas are very ripe. The cinnamon-walnut topping is optional but worth the thirty seconds it takes.

## Ingredients

### Bread

- 1/4 lb (1 stick) cool butter, plus more for greasing the pan
- 3/4 cup dark brown sugar
- 2 eggs, at room temperature
- 2 1/3 cups very ripe bananas (about 5)
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1/4 tsp salt

### Topping

- 3 tbsp chopped walnuts or pecans
- 1 tbsp granulated or coarse sugar
- 1/8 tsp cinnamon

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven** to 175°C (350°F). Lightly grease a 9-by-5-inch loaf pan.

2. **Cream the butter.** Using an electric mixer, beat the butter until smooth and fluffy. Add the brown sugar and cream together for 2 minutes more. Mix in the eggs one at a time. Add the bananas and mix until only small lumps remain — you want some texture, not a smooth puree.

3. **Combine the dry ingredients.** Stir the flour, baking soda, and salt together in a separate bowl. Mix into the banana mixture just until combined. The moment the flour disappears, stop. Overmixing develops gluten and gives you a tough, dense loaf instead of a tender one.

4. **Top and bake.** Pour the batter into the prepared pan. If making the topping, stir the walnuts, sugar, and cinnamon together and sprinkle evenly over the batter. Bake for 55-65 minutes, until a toothpick inserted into the centre comes out clean.

5. **Cool.** Let the bread sit in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack and cool completely before slicing. It slices cleaner when fully cool, and the flavour deepens as it rests.

## Notes

- **Ripe bananas are non-negotiable.** You want bananas with heavy brown spotting on the skin — the uglier the better. Underripe bananas lack the sweetness and moisture that make banana bread work. Frozen bananas are ideal: peel before freezing, thaw completely, and drain off any excess liquid.
- **Don't overmix.** This is the most common mistake. Once the flour is added, fold gently and stop the moment you can't see dry streaks. A few small lumps are fine.
- **Room temperature eggs.** Cold eggs can cause the creamed butter to seize and curdle. If you forgot to take them out, sit them in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Storage.** Wraps tightly in cling film or foil once completely cool. Keeps 3-4 days at room temperature, or freeze whole for up to 3 months. Slice before freezing if you want to pull individual pieces.
