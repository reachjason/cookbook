# Banana Oat Pancakes

Three ingredients, no flour, and genuinely good — not good-for-you good, but actually good. The trick is resting the batter. Blending the oats with banana and egg gives you a loose, unpromising-looking mixture. Fifteen minutes later the oats have absorbed the moisture and the batter holds together on the pan like it was always meant to. Cook them low and slow. These brown faster than flour pancakes because of the natural sugar in the banana, and the centre needs time to set through. Patience is the only technique required here.

## Ingredients

### Batter

- 2 ripe bananas (brown spots on the skin, but not black and mushy — overripe bananas release too much water and the pancakes won't hold)
- 2 large eggs
- 1/2 cup rolled oats (80g — not instant, not steel-cut)
- 1/2 tsp baking powder
- pinch of fine sea salt

### To Serve

- maple syrup
- fresh berries, sliced banana, or seasonal fruit
- natural yoghurt (optional)

## Instructions

1. **Blend the batter.** Add the peeled bananas, eggs, oats, baking powder, and salt to a blender. Blitz for 20-30 seconds until smooth. A few small oat flecks are fine — they add texture. If you prefer a completely smooth pancake, blend for a full minute.

2. **Rest.** Pour the batter into a bowl and leave it to sit for 15 minutes at room temperature. This is not optional. The oats need time to hydrate and swell, which thickens the batter from a pourable liquid into something that actually holds its shape on the pan. Skip this and you will have flat, fragile crepes that fall apart when you flip them.

3. **Heat the pan.** Set a non-stick frying pan over medium-low heat — lower than you think. Let it warm for a full minute. Add the lightest spray of oil or wipe the surface with a paper towel dipped in a neutral oil. You want a film, not a puddle.

4. **Cook.** Spoon roughly 2 tablespoons of batter per pancake onto the pan. Do not spread it — let it find its own shape. Cook for 2-3 minutes until you see small bubbles forming across the surface and the edges look set and slightly dry. These are your signals. Flip gently with a thin spatula. Cook for another 1-2 minutes until the underside is golden. The second side always cooks faster.

5. **Repeat.** Wipe the pan between batches if any residue builds up. Adjust the heat down if the pancakes are browning before the centre sets — the banana sugar caramelises quickly.

6. **Serve.** Stack on a warm plate. Drizzle with maple syrup, scatter with fresh fruit, and add a spoonful of yoghurt if you like. These are best eaten immediately — they don't sit well.

## Notes

- **Oat type matters.** Rolled oats (old-fashioned) are the only kind that work properly here. They blend smooth but retain enough structure to bind the batter. Instant oats dissolve too much and give you a thin, wet pancake. Steel-cut oats won't break down in a blender.
- **Banana ripeness.** You want ripe — yellow with brown freckles. This gives you natural sweetness and a batter that blends easily. Underripe bananas are starchy and bland. Overripe bananas (black skin, mushy flesh) release too much liquid and the pancakes won't hold together no matter how long you rest the batter.
- **The rest is everything.** Fifteen minutes transforms the batter. If you are in a rush, ten minutes will do. Five will not.
- **Heat control.** Medium-low, not medium. The banana sugars mean these brown at a lower temperature than regular pancakes. If the outside is golden but the inside is still wet, your pan is too hot. Turn it down and give the centre time to cook through.
- **Freezing.** These freeze well for up to 3 months. Cool completely on a wire rack, then stack with strips of baking paper between each pancake. Reheat from frozen in a toaster — two cycles on a medium setting.
- **Gluten-free.** Naturally gluten-free as written, but only if your oats are certified gluten-free. Standard oats are often cross-contaminated with wheat during processing.
